Portmanteau Lemma

The Portmanteau Lemma is a collection of deceptively simple results that quietly underpins much of modern asymptotic theory. The name portmanteau (its adjective definition, “consisting of a wide range of things that are considered as a single thing”) is apt: the lemma gathers several equivalent formulations of weak convergence of probability measures. It’s houses a lot of technical machinery that lets us move freely between convergence of probabilities, expectations, and indicator functions.

Portmanteau Lemma (as per van der Vaart).
Let \(X_n\) and \(X\) be random elements taking values in a metric space \((\mathcal{X}, d)\). The following are equivalent:

  1. \(X_n \xrightarrow{d} X\), i.e., \(\P(X_n \in A) \to \P(X \in A)\) for all continuity points of \(x \mapsto \P(X \leq x)\);
  2. \(\E[f(X_n)] \to \E[f(X)]\) for all bounded, continuous functions \(f\);
  3. \(\E[f(X_n)] \to \E[f(X)]\) for all bounded, Lipschitz functions \(f\);
  4. \(\lim \inf \E[f(X_n)] \geq \E[f(X)]\) for all nonnegative, continuous functions \(f\);
  5. \(\lim \inf \P(X_n \in G) \geq \P(X \in G)\) for every open set \(G\);
  6. \(\lim \sup \P(X_n \in F) \leq \P(X \in F)\) for every closed set \(F\);
  7. \(\P(X_n \in B) \to \P(X \in B)\) for all Borel sets \(B\) with \(\P(X \in \partial B) = 0\) where \(\partial B\) is the boundary of \(B\).

Proofs

(1) This is the definition of weak convergence (or convergence in distribution). In effect the rest imply that they are equivalent conditions. Note, the phrasing “for all continuity points” means “the \(x\) where the limiting CDF \(F_X(x) = \P(X \leq x)\) is continuous”. This is sufficient for convergence in distribution and avoids pathologies at discontinuities.
