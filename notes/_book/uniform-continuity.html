Becoming a Theory Wonk: Notes, Proofs and Pain

Uniform Continuity

Let \(f : \mathbb{X} \to \mathbb{Y}\) be a function between metric spaces \((\mathbb{X}, d_1)\) and \((\mathbb{Y}, d_2)\), where \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) measure distance over the input and output spaces respectively.

The function \(f\) is uniformly continuous if, for every \(\epsilon > 0\), there exists some \(\delta > 0\) such that for all \(x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{X}\), \[ d_1(x_1, x_2) < \delta \quad \Rightarrow \quad d_2(f(x_1), f(x_2)) < \epsilon. \]

I sometimes think of uniform continuity as box continuity: for any box of height \(\epsilon\) centred at any point \(x \in \mathbb{X}\), there exists a width \(\delta\) such that the graph of \(f\) always exits the box through the sides, never the top. Wikipedia has a nice graphic of this.

Example: Portmanteau Lemma

Uniform continuity often appears when we want to bound terms of the form
\[ |\mathbb{E}[f(X)] - \mathbb{E}[f_\epsilon(X)]|, \] where \(f_\epsilon\) is a piecewise approximation of \(f\). This trick turns integrals into finite sums.

For example, part of the proof of the Portmanteau Lemma relies on this kind of bound. Choose a sufficiently large compact rectangle \(I \subset \mathbb{X}\) such that \(\mathrm{P}(X \notin I) < \epsilon\). By uniform continuity, for any given \(\epsilon > 0\) there exists a \(\delta > 0\) such that whenever \(d_1(x_1, x_2) < \delta\), we have \(|f(x_1) - f(x_2)| < \epsilon\). Because this \(\delta\) works uniformly across all \(x \in \mathbb{X}\), we can use compactness of \(I\) to cover it with finitely many open \(\delta/2\)-balls. Refining this finite cover to a disjoint collection of sets \(\{I_j\}\) (for instance, by successive set subtraction) yields a finite partition of \(I\) such that \(f\) varies by at most \(\epsilon\) on each \(I_j\). Define
\[ f_\epsilon(x) = \sum_j f(x_j) \mathbf{1}_{I_j}(x), \] for any representative \(x_j \in I_j\). If \(f\) is bounded by some constant \(M\), then \(|f(x) - f_\epsilon(x)| < \epsilon\) on \(I\), and we can bound \[\begin{align*} |\mathbb{E}[f(X)] - \mathbb{E}[f_\epsilon(X)]| &= \left| \int_{\mathbb{X}} f(x) - f_\epsilon(x) \, \mathrm{d}P(x) \right| \\ &= \left| \int_{I^c} f(x) - f_\epsilon(x) \, \mathrm{d}P(x) + \int_{I} f(x) - f_\epsilon(x) \, \mathrm{d}P(x) \right| \\ &\leq \left| \int_{I^c} f(x) - f_\epsilon(x) \, \mathrm{d}P(x)\right| + \left|\int_{I} f(x) - f_\epsilon(x) \, \mathrm{d}P(x) \right| \\ &\leq M \mathrm{P}(X \notin I) + \sum_j \int_{I_j} |f(x) - f_\epsilon(x)|\, \mathrm{d}P(x) \\ &\leq M \mathrm{P}(X \notin I) + \epsilon \sum_j \mathrm{P}(X \in I_j) \\ &\leq (M+1)\epsilon. \end{align*}\]
